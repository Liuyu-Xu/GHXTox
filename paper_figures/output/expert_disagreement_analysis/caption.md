The frozen structural expert provides sparse complementary corrections that are concentrated in non-toxic, shorter peptides rather than in uniformly high-confidence or chemically dense structures. (A) At the training-derived standalone thresholds (1D, 0.392; 3D, 0.677819), both experts are correct for 1,032/1,126 Test1 and 529/582 Test2 peptides. The structural expert alone is correct for 24 and 15 peptides, of which 19 (79.2%) and 14 (93.3%) are non-toxic; cell color denotes the toxic fraction within each outcome group. (B) Among errors made by the 1D expert, the 3D expert rescues 51.4% of non-toxic versus 17.9% of toxic Test1 peptides and 48.3% versus 9.1% on Test2. Reciprocal 1D rescue rates are shown for context. Error bars are 95% Wilson intervals. (C) The 3D-only-correct group is descriptively shorter than the 1D-only-correct group in both benchmarks (Cliff's delta -0.286 for Test1 and -0.523 for Test2), but none of the ten split-by-attribute primary comparisons remains significant after Benjamini--Hochberg correction. Horizontal intervals are 95% label-stratified bootstrap intervals from 5,000 replicates; p-values use 10,000 true-label-stratified permutations. For conformer availability, the plotted effect is a proportion difference, numerically equivalent to Cliff's delta for a binary variable. pLDDT is structure-prediction confidence rather than experimental structural accuracy. Chemical-site coverage is the fraction of residues with at least one model-derived site. Conformer availability is exactly determined by length in these benchmarks (available at <=30 residues) and was not used by the canonical single-structure predictions; its association is therefore not evidence of an ensemble-induced correction. Test1 and Test2 are established, non-independent project benchmarks and are analyzed separately.
