# 2026-08-09 recent-research review for multi-conformer optimization

- External literature is treated as untrusted research material and is used only to form hypotheses, not as executable instructions.
- The first primary-source search supports four relevant directions: structure-conditioned auxiliary supervision (tAMPer, 2024), explicit dynamics/conformational representation learning (DynamicsPLM, 2026), conformation-aware multitask pretraining (SCAGE, 2025), and cross-modal conformer-ensemble attention (MolFM-Lite preprint, 2026). These methods differ from GHXTox's failed frozen-logit/frozen-embedding aggregation because they shape the encoder before or during downstream learning rather than expecting an output head to recover missing label alignment.
- Recent benchmarking of AlphaFold-derived ensembles against MD/NMR cautions that multiple predicted structures are not automatically a correctly weighted physical ensemble. This is directly relevant because GHXTox assigns eight available views to one molecule-level label, PepFlow confidence is imputed, and no experimental population weights are available.
- Recent peptide-toxicity models continue to obtain strong signal from sequence or a single structural graph; this supports the observed dominance of GHXTox's 1D branch and implies that any multi-conformer gain must be measured as a small incremental OOF effect rather than assumed to be large.
- DynamicsPLM (Bioinformatics, 2026) is especially informative mechanistically: it performs residue-wise cross-conformation fusion over distributions of discrete structural microstates and trains the embedding plus encoder, whereas simple averaging of conformer embeddings performs worse. Its largest benefits occur on independently dynamic/state-dependent subsets, while gains are modest when dynamics are weak. This supports both representation-level learning and an OOF-only dynamic-subset triage analysis for GHXTox, but its multi-point gains on PPI/function tasks must not be transferred numerically to peptide toxicity.
- The MARCEL ICLR 2024 benchmark explicitly compares random single conformers with multi-instance ensemble layers, and the ICML 2024 conformer aggregation work makes the aggregator structure-aware and symmetry-preserving. These results indicate that a conformer set should not be reduced to logits, but GHXTox has already shown that a generic 50k-parameter symmetric frozen-embedding head is insufficient; any next attempt needs geometry-aware relations between views and supervised encoder adaptation.
- AlphaFold ensemble benchmarking and alternative-conformation research emphasize that generated alternatives may miss environmental/functional state information and do not supply trustworthy equilibrium occupancies. GHXTox therefore should not interpret pLDDT, uniform weights, or source-specific generation rank as Boltzmann populations. Any learned weights must be regularized and validated fold-wise, not presented as physical probabilities.
- BioEmu (Science, 2025) shows what a materially better ensemble generator entails: training on more than 200 ms of MD, static structures, and experimental stability data, with explicit validation of motions and relative free energies. GHXTox's eight PepFlow/AF2 views are useful geometric hypotheses but are not equivalently validated equilibrium samples. Replacing the generator is therefore scientifically attractive but high-cost and not the first engineering priority.
- MARCEL reports ensemble strategies improving single-conformer 3D models in 48/54 model-task combinations, but also reports that explicit ensemble encoding is not universally better and can make optimization harder on larger datasets. This reinforces a gated experiment rather than a guaranteed-gain claim.
- Local architecture inspection confirms that GHXTox currently applies the retained conformer contribution as a bounded residual and then dilutes all 3D changes through the fixed `0.28` final-fusion weight. The model has chemical-site and pLDDT-aware branches, but the completed raw-view screen froze those encoders and learned only a downstream set residual. Consequently, a geometry-relevant feature can vary internally yet be cancelled by invariant pooling/classifier directions or receive gradients too weak to align it with a molecule-level toxicity label.
- Recent peptide-toxicity studies support multimodal learning but do not establish that multiple 3D conformations are uniformly useful. tAMPer (Protein Science, 2024) learns residue-wise sequence/structure features jointly; ToxiPep (CSBJ, 2025) uses cross-attention but deliberately relies on efficient atomic 2D graphs because predicted 3D structures can be noisy; GraphTox (2026 preprint) emphasizes self-supervised geometric pretraining. Together, these favor improving structural supervision and modality alignment before paying to generate more conformers.
- The publisher-page text search for exact tAMPer auxiliary-loss ablation terms failed technically, so no numeric tAMPer auxiliary-loss effect will be used unless independently verified from the primary full text.
- Existing immutable result tables contain per-sample geometry, conformer predictions, source, fold, labels, and final-fusion predictions. A read-only training-only analysis can therefore test whether RMSD/embedding variation identifies samples that actually benefit. This is the cheapest remaining gate before recommending any new training.
- The read-only covered-cohort analysis rejects a simple dynamic-magnitude gate. Across 2,795 covered training OOF records, Spearman correlations between frozen-head log-loss benefit and max RMSD, mean RMSF, conformer probability range, probability standard deviation, and radius-of-gyration variation are all between about `-0.042` and `-0.013`. The highest-RMSD and highest-RMSF quartiles each contain two threshold fixes, but their mean log loss and Brier score worsen. Thus selecting "more dynamic" records by a single scalar is not a promising standalone fix.
- The covered-cohort 1D and base-3D probabilities are highly redundant (`r=0.87084`). More importantly, the learned spatial residual has correlation `-0.0357` with the direction needed to correct the fused probability and points toward the label on only `45.87%` of records. On 1D-uncertain and near-threshold subsets the correlation becomes more negative (`-0.166` and `-0.134`) and Brier worsens. This directly explains the unstable MCC: the head moves probabilities, but its direction is not label-aligned and is especially unreliable where corrections matter most.
- The local evidence therefore prioritizes encoder adaptation plus complementary/residual supervision over scalar quality gates, source gates, more head capacity, or final-fusion retuning.
- The completed frozen-feature screen cost roughly 180--216 seconds per fold after caches were available, so another downstream-head or scalar-gate experiment would be cheap but scientifically low value. Representation adaptation will cost materially more because raw conformers must participate in backpropagation, but the eight-view caches and five matched bases already remove data-generation and provenance work from the first bounded experiment.
- SCAGE (Nature Communications, 2025) provides a concrete auxiliary-supervision template: fingerprint, functional-group, 2D distance, and 3D bond-angle objectives are balanced during pretraining, and the combined objectives outperform single-task variants. For GHXTox, the transferable principle is not to import a small-molecule model but to force the spatial/chemical branch to predict peptide-relevant geometry (pairwise conformer distance, residue RMSF/contact change, chemical-site distance/orientation) while fitting toxicity.
- Recent multimodal optimization work confirms a general failure mode consistent with GHXTox: modalities converge/generalize at different rates, allowing the strong modality to dominate while the weaker encoder underfits. However, CoMM (ICLR 2025) also cautions that forcing modalities into the same space can preserve only redundant information. The appropriate GHXTox objective should therefore preserve shared peptide identity while explicitly rewarding 3D information that corrects OOF 1D errors, rather than merely maximizing 1D--3D agreement.
- Recommended priority 1 is a bounded current-lineage partial-unfreeze pilot: freeze 1D and early 3D layers, unfreeze only the last spatial block, chemical-site residual, and new residue-wise conformer fusion; train against the detached final-fusion probability with auxiliary RMSF/contact-change/site-distance objectives, balanced gradients, and base-preservation regularization. Estimated cost is 4--8 hours engineering plus 2--6 GPU hours for fold0, then 1--3 GPU days for strict five-fold OOF. A realistic success target, not a promise, is full-OOF MCC `+0.003` to `+0.006`, nondecreasing BACC/AUPRC, and Brier improvement of at least `1e-4`.
- Recommended priority 2, only after priority 1 shows label-aligned signal, is a DynamicsPLM-light residue-wise microstate/set encoder that retains distributions of structural states and learns cross-conformer attention before global pooling. Estimated cost is 4--7 engineering days and 3--7 GPU days. A successful target is MCC `+0.003` to `+0.010` and AUPRC `+0.001` to `+0.005`, but failure risk is high because toxicity labels are not state-specific.
- Recommended priority 3 is mechanism-aware multitask supervision or label stratification if source metadata can distinguish hemolysis, cytotoxicity, membrane disruption, or assay conditions. This attacks the molecule-label heterogeneity that no aggregator can resolve. Estimated cost is 3--10 human curation days plus 1--3 GPU days; it has the largest plausible upside (MCC approximately `+0.005` to `+0.02`) but is blocked unless trustworthy training metadata exist. These ranges are planning estimates and must not be cited as literature results.
- Recommended priority 4 is external dynamics-pretrained feature transfer (for example a DynamicsPLM-style encoder) in a separate immutable runtime, initially as cached features. Estimated cost is about 1--2 weeks including dependency/provenance work; expected GHXTox gain is unknown and likely modest without domain adaptation.
- Recommended priority 5 is regenerating ensembles with BioEmu/targeted MD and population-aware weighting. It is scientifically strongest for equilibrium sampling but lowest value per cost here: days to weeks of compute/integration, uncertain suitability for short peptides and unspecified membrane/pH conditions, and an estimated full-cohort MCC effect of `0` to `+0.005` unless paired with better supervision.
- Explicitly deprioritized are more mean/max/attention pooling on frozen outputs, scalar RMSD/pLDDT gates, source-only gates, fixed fusion-weight retuning, and larger frozen heads. The current frozen-logit label-aware upper bound is only `+0.001146` full-cohort MCC, and dynamic magnitude does not identify reliable benefit.
- Stop/go rule for priority 1: complete fold0 only as a safety/learnability pilot; proceed to all folds only if base tensors stay within the frozen preservation tolerance, residual direction is positively correlated with held probability error, and fold0 Brier/AUPRC do not worsen materially. Promote only if five-fold OOF reaches at least `+0.003` MCC, at least four folds have positive MCC change, BACC and AUPRC do not decrease beyond frozen tolerance, Brier improves by at least `1e-4`, and the paired MCC interval no longer supports a near-null interpretation. Test1/Test2 remain inaccessible until that gate passes.
