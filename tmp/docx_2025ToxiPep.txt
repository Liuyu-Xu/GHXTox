Keywords:
Peptide-based therapeutics have emerged as a promising avenue in drug development, oﬀering high biocom-
patibility, speciﬁcity, and eﬃcacy. However, the potential toxicity of peptides remains a signiﬁcant challenge,
necessitating the development of robust toxicity prediction methods. In this study, we introduce ToxiPep, a novel
dual-model framework for peptide toxicity prediction that integrates sequence-based contextual information with
atomic-level structural features. This framework combines BiGRU and Transformer to capture local and global
sequence dependencies while leveraging multi-scale CNNs to extract reﬁned structural features from molecu-
lar graphs derived from peptide SMILES representations. A cross-attention mechanism aligns and fuses these
two feature modalities, enabling the model to capture intricate relationships between sequence and structural
information. ToxiPep outperforms several state-of-the-art tools, including ToxinPred2, CSM-Toxin, PepNet, and
ToxinPred3, on both internal and independent test sets. Additionally, interpretability analyses reveal that ToxiPep
identiﬁes key amino acids along with their structural features, providing insights into the molecular mechanisms
of peptide toxicity. To facilitate broader accessibility, we have also developed a web server for convenient user
access. Overall, this framework has the potential to accelerate the identiﬁcation of safer therapeutic peptides,
oﬀering new opportunities for peptide-based drug development in precision medicine.

In this study, we developed ToxiPep, a dual-model framework for
peptide toxicity prediction that integrates context-aware representations
and atomic-level graph features to enhance prediction accuracy and ro-
bustness. The framework, as shown in Fig. 1, begins by sequence embed-
dings, which are processed through a bidirectional GRU (BiGRU) layer
and Transformer encoder blocks to capture both local and global depen-
dencies. Simultaneously, atomic-level graph features are extracted from
the SMILES representation of peptides by constructing molecular graphs.
These features are further reﬁned through multiple 2D convolutional
layers (Multi-scale CNN) to produce a detailed structural representa-
tion. The context-aware representations and atomic-level graph features
are then fused through a cross-attention mechanism, aligning relevant
information between the two modalities. The fused representation is
passed through a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) classiﬁer to predict pep-
tide toxicity. This hybrid approach eﬀectively leverages both contextual
and structural information, providing a powerful computational tool for
peptide toxicity prediction. Additionally, we developed a web server
to facilitate user-friendly access to ToxiPep, allowing researchers to ef-
ﬁciently predict peptide toxicity through an intuitive online platform,
available at https://awi.cuhk.edu.cn/~dbAMP/ToxiPep/.
Despite signiﬁcant advancements in peptide toxicity prediction,
there are still notable limitations. First, most models are not speciﬁcally
designed for short peptides, which are critical in many applications.
For instance, peptides with a length of 10-50 amino acids often ex-
hibit higher bioactivity and tissue penetration capabilities, while their
lower molecular weight reduces immunogenicity and enhances drug
targeting speciﬁcity [26,27]. Furthermore, the length range of these
peptides facilitates the formation of stable secondary structures, which
are closely associated with their biological functions [28]. Second, many
current toxicity prediction methods primarily rely on sequence-based
features, such as amino acid composition or sequence motifs, while
neglecting molecular structural information, potentially limiting their
ability to capture key toxicity-related mechanisms. Third, existing mod-
els often lack suﬃcient interpretability, making it challenging to derive
insights into the underlying mechanisms of peptide toxicity. By integrat-
ing both sequence and structural features, a deeper understanding of the
mechanisms of peptide toxicity could be achieved, leading to improved
predictive reliability and broader applicability. Moreover, recent studies
have shown that some models may fail to generalize beyond the training
data, resulting in inconsistent or inaccurate predictions when applied to
newly designed peptides. For example, Chenopodin-derived peptides
exhibited negligible haemolysis in in vitro assays despite favorable
Overall, this framework not only improves predictive accuracy and
robustness but also deepens the understanding of the molecular mech-
anisms underlying peptide toxicity. The insights gained from this study
have the potential to positively inﬂuence peptide-based therapeutic de-
sign, enabling the identiﬁcation of safer and more eﬀective therapeutic
peptides.

will not only help validate the practical utility of ToxiPep but also sup-
port iterative model improvement through data feedback.

types of features.
3.6. ToxiPep web server for fast and accessible toxicity prediction
For user convenience, our framework is implemented as a user-
friendly web interface (Fig. 7), freely accessible at https://awi.cuhk.
edu.cn/~dbAMP/ToxiPep/. Users can start by clicking the “Start Pre-
diction” button, which opens a page to input peptide sequences directly
or upload a text ﬁle containing multiple sequences. Upon submission,
the system processes the input and displays the predicted toxicity re-
sults, including toxicity classiﬁcation and conﬁdence scores from the
model in an intuitive format. The web server is capable of predicting 10
peptide sequences in an average of 5.4 seconds and supports batch sub-
missions of up to 1,000 sequences, enabling eﬃcient high-throughput
analysis.
Second, our study focuses on binary classiﬁcation of peptides as toxic
or non-toxic, rather than estimating toxicity potency in a continuous
manner (e.g., IC values). The toxicity labels were assigned based on

0
curated annotations in established databases such as DRAMP4.0 and
dbAMP3.0, which reﬂect experimental observations or literature-based
classiﬁcation, rather than uniformly deﬁned potency thresholds. There-
fore, the model is not designed to predict or classify peptides based on
IC cutoﬀs.
