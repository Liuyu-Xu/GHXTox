GHXTox: Peptide toxicity prediction by fusing task-adaptive
 protein language model with pLDDT-aware multi-scale 3D
                   structural modeling
               Liuyu Xu𝑎 , Kehan Gao𝑎 , Lechen Hao𝑎 , Shengli Zhang𝑎,∗
𝑎 School of Mathematics and Statistics, Xidian University, Xi’an 710071, P. R. China

Abstract. Peptide toxicity is a major safety concern in peptide-based drug development,
motivating accurate computational screening methods. Existing methods have begun
to explore multi-dimensional (multimodal) feature fusion and toxicity prediction based
on the three-dimensional (3D) conformations of peptides, yet most of them still un-
derutilize 3D structural information, and systematic multi-dimensional fusion remains
rare. Here, we propose GHXTox, a hierarchical dual-level fusion framework in which a
3D structural expert first integrates sequence semantics with spatial structural features
through predicted local-distance difference test (pLDDT)-aware cross-attention, and is
then fused at the probability level with task-adaptive one-dimensional (1D) sequence
expert using weights frozen on training out-of-fold (OOF) predictions. The 1D branch
analyzes sequences with a protein language model (ProtT5 fine-tuned with low-rank
adaptation, LoRA) and a bidirectional Mamba state-space model. The 3D expert inte-
grates an ESMFold-derived structural anchor, pLDDT-aware residue geometry, and full-
atom-derived chemical sites. A separate residual head was trained on multi-conformer
descriptors while the single-structure backbone was frozen. This residual is disabled
in the canonical predictor and is reported only in controlled ablations; benchmark and
public inference use the single-structure base logits. On the frozen historical bench-
mark test sets, GHXTox achieves MCC values of 0.8705 and 0.6673 and AUPRC values
of 0.9674 and 0.8046 on Test1 and Test2, respectively. Further analyses characterize
residue-level sequence sensitivity, confidence–gate associations, chemical-site pertur-
bations, expert disagreement, and an OOF-frozen selective-prediction rule. At realized
coverages of 88.54% and 86.94%, the selective rule reduced the accepted-sample error
to 2.71% and 3.16% on Test1 and Test2 without benchmark-specific cutoff selection.
These findings are interpreted as model-level evidence rather than causal biological
mechanisms. Frozen-model analyses indicate that the conformer contribution is small
and non-uniform: under the structural expert, substantial geometric variation does not
necessarily translate into stable outcome-aligned probability variation.
Keywords: peptide toxicity prediction; multimodal deep learning; protein language
model; bidirectional Mamba; pLDDT-aware multi-scale 3D modeling; interpretability
