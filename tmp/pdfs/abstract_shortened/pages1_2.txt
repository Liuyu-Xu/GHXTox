GHXTox: Peptide toxicity prediction by fusing task-adaptive
 protein language model with pLDDT-aware multi-scale 3D
                   structural modeling
              Liuyu Xu𝑎 , Kehan Gao𝑎 , Lechen Hao𝑎 , Shengli Zhang𝑎,∗
𝑎 School of Mathematics and Statistics, Xidian University, Xi’an 710071, P. R. China
∗ Corresponding author. Email: shengli0201@163.com




Abstract. Peptide toxicity is a major safety concern in peptide-based drug develop-
ment, motivating accurate computational screening. Existing predictors often underuse
three-dimensional (3D) structural information and provide limited cross-modal integra-
tion. We propose GHXTox, a hierarchical dual-level fusion framework that combines
task-adaptive ProtT5–Bi-Mamba sequence modeling with pLDDT-aware multi-scale
structural modeling. The structural expert integrates sequence semantics, residue ge-
ometry, and full-atom-derived chemical sites, and its probability is fused with that of
the sequence expert using weights frozen from training out-of-fold predictions. On two
frozen historical benchmarks, GHXTox achieved MCC values of 0.8705 and 0.6673
and AUPRC values of 0.9674 and 0.8046 on test set 1 and test set 2, respectively.
The two experts provided complementary evidence, while residue- and structure-level
analyses supported model interpretability. An OOF-frozen selective-prediction rule re-
duced accepted-sample error from 5.24% to 2.71% and from 5.33% to 3.16% at realized
coverages of 88.54% and 86.94%, respectively. These retrospective results support
confidence-aware sequence–structure fusion for peptide toxicity prediction and require
confirmation on newly prefrozen external data.
Keywords: peptide toxicity prediction; multimodal deep learning; protein language
model; bidirectional Mamba; pLDDT-aware multi-scale 3D modeling; interpretability

1 INTRODUCTION
    Peptides are increasingly used as therapeutic candidates because of their high target
specificity and functional diversity [1]. However, peptide toxicity remains a major
safety concern during drug development, and the accurate and efficient identification
of toxic peptides is a major bottleneck in peptide-based drug discovery [2]. Because
conventional experimental toxicity assays are costly, time-consuming, and difficult to
scale, reliable computational predictors are highly desirable [2].
    Computational peptide toxicity prediction has evolved from traditional machine
learning methods based on handcrafted physicochemical features toward deep learn-
ing approaches with stronger representation-learning capability. Current mainstream
methods can be roughly grouped into two categories. The first category operates on
sequence-level (one-dimensional, 1D) and/or atomic-level (two-dimensional, 2D) rep-
resentations. For example, ToxMSRC combines continuous bag-of-words (CBOW) em-
beddings, multi-scale convolutional neural networks, and a bidirectional long short-term
memory network with residual connections, achieving strong performance on the bench-
mark dataset [2]. ToxPLTC integrates pooled ProtT5 embeddings, borderline-SMOTE
augmentation, and a text convolutional neural network [3]. ToxiPep fuses context-aware
sequence representations with atomic-level graphs through a cross-attention mecha-
nism [4]. HyPepTox-Fuse further combines embeddings from multiple protein language
models with conventional descriptors [5]. Although these models achieve strong per-
formance within their respective modalities, they largely neglect the three-dimensional
(3D) conformations of peptides, which are closely related to toxicity; cross-dimensional
information interaction and mutual validation remain limited, and their interpretability
is mostly confined to the sequence level.
    The second category attempts to exploit 3D structural information. PeptiTox uses
ESM2 sequence embeddings together with ESMFold-predicted 3D structures and ge-
ometric graph neural networks [6]. StrucToxNet feeds ProtT5 sequence embeddings
and ESMFold-predicted structures into an equivariant graph neural network (EGNN)
and systematically analyzes structural quality [7]. These studies demonstrate the value
of 3D conformations for toxicity prediction. However, these predictors still depend on
predicted structures whose reliability can vary for short peptides, and both evaluate one
predicted conformation per sequence rather than an explicit conformer ensemble. Exist-
ing designs use predicted-structure confidence primarily for filtering or structural-quality
analysis rather than as a continuous message-level reliability signal [6–8]. Moreover, the
fusion between the sequence and structure branches remains relatively shallow, and no
systematic multi-dimensional fusion that combines task-adaptive sequence model with
confidence-aware multi-scale structure modeling has been established.
    To address these limitations, we propose GHXTox, an interpretable hierarchical dual-
level fusion framework for peptide toxicity prediction. Task-adaptive 1D sequence expert
combines a ProtT5 protein language model, adapted by low-rank adaptation (LoRA),
with a bidirectional Mamba state-space encoder and three-seed ensembling [9–11].
