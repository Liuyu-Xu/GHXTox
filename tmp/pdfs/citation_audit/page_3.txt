experiments on the frozen historical benchmark test sets show that GHXTox improves several
fixed-threshold and probability-quality measures on Test1 while maintaining broadly comparable
performance on Test2, while ablation, robustness, and interpretability analyses characterize the
conditions under which sequence and structural evidence are complementary.


2     MATERIALS AND METHODS
2.1   Datasets
      We evaluate GHXTox on the benchmark datasets constructed by CAPTP [17] and subse-
quently adopted by ToxMSRC [2]. Following the data construction protocol of ToxMSRC [2],
toxic peptide sequences were merged from three databases, namely CSM-Toxin [18], Toxin-
Pred2 [19], and ATSE [20], and sequences longer than 50 residues were removed. Non-toxic
sequences were retrieved from UniProt [21] using the query “NOT KW-0800 AND NOT KW-
0020 AND reviewed:true”, and sequences containing non-standard amino acids or exceeding
50 residues were excluded. After combining the two groups and removing samples with con-
flicting labels, CD-HIT [22] was applied with a sequence-identity threshold of 0.9 to eliminate
redundant sequences, yielding 2,138 toxic and 5,375 non-toxic peptides. Following the same
partition as ToxMSRC [2], we used 85% of the data as the training set and the remaining
15% as the benchmark Test1 set, resulting in 1,818 toxic and 4,569 non-toxic training peptides
and 320 toxic and 806 non-toxic Test1 peptides. An additional benchmark Test2 set, contain-
ing 46 toxic and 536 non-toxic peptides, is used for frozen historical evaluation. Both Test1
and Test2 had been observed during historical project development and are therefore treated
as frozen historical benchmarks rather than newly blinded external validation sets; their la-
bels were not used in the formal OOF calibration, fusion-weight selection, or threshold-freezing
procedure. ToxinPred 3.0 [23] was consulted as an external method and data-source reference
during project development, but its records were not added to the present training set or to the
formal Test1/Test2 result tables. Table 1 summarizes the statistics of the datasets used in this
study.

2.2   Overview of the Model
     GHXTox is a hierarchical dual-level fusion framework for peptide toxicity prediction: a task-
adaptive one-dimensional (1D) sequence expert and a sequence-enhanced three-dimensional (3D)
structural expert generate independent probabilities, interact internally at the feature level, and
are combined at the probability level.
     The 1D expert processes the peptide sequence with a LoRA-adapted ProtT5 encoder [9,10]
followed by a bidirectional Mamba state-space model [11], averages three seed models, and
applies OOF-fitted Platt calibration to produce p1D (Section 2.3). The 3D expert integrates
sequence semantics, residue geometry, full-atom chemical sites, and pLDDT confidence across
residue, local-spatial, chemical-site, and peptide scales, combines sequence and spatial states
through pLDDT-aware cross-attention, and emits pSE3D (Section 2.4).
     The two probabilities are combined at the probability level; the fusion rule, the decision
threshold, and the final output are described in Section 2.5.
