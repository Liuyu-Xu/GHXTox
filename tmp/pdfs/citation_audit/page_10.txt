enlarges the receptive field hop by hop: the first layer primarily encodes direct geometric neigh-

message passing. GHXTox uses three layers, a depth that balances context integration against
model complexity and overfitting risk on short and uncertain predicted peptide structures. Un-
like approaches that simply append pLDDT as an ordinary node feature, GHXTox uses pLDDT
to directly modulate the strength of geometric messages, allowing structural uncertainty to in-
fluence representation learning at the propagation stage. Low pLDDT does not imply that a
residue is biologically unimportant; it may reflect prediction error or genuine conformational
flexibility, so the model reduces the reliability of uncertain coordinate evidence while preserving
the residue’s sequence-semantic and physicochemical features.

2.4.4   Full-Atom Chemical-Site Message Passing
     A Cα-based residue graph captures the global fold and spatial neighborhoods but cannot
resolve chemically distinct moieties within the same side chain. For example, tyrosine contains
both an aromatic ring and a hydroxyl group, whereas tryptophan contains an aromatic system
and an indole nitrogen. Compressing each side chain into a single centroid would mix these
chemically different moieties. GHXTox therefore extracts chemistry-aware pseudo-sites from the
predicted full-atom PDB structure and processes them with a dedicated local message-passing
branch [28].
     To retain this chemical detail, up to two chemical sites are kept for each residue. Site identity
is represented by an eight-dimensional multi-hot vector tir ∈ {0, 1}8 allowing overlapping types:
positive, negative, donor, acceptor, aromatic, hydrophobic, sulfur, and conformational, where r
indexes the chemical site within residue i. Multiple types may be assigned to the same site: for
example, the cysteine sulfur site is labeled as donor, hydrophobic, and sulfur-containing, whereas
the proline ring is labeled as hydrophobic and conformationally constrained; the aromatic ring
and hydroxyl group of tyrosine are represented as separate sites, as are the aromatic system and
the indole NE1 nitrogen (donor) of tryptophan.

mean position of the heavy atoms defining the corresponding chemical moiety, and its orientation
is represented by either a bond direction or an unsigned plane normal. Because the sign of a plane
normal is arbitrary, downstream directional features use absolute cosines, preserving rotation
invariance and eliminating orientation-sign ambiguity. Combining these attributes, the initial
