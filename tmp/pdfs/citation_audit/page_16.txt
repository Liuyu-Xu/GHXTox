3.2   Complementarity of the 1D and 3D Experts
    The two experts are fused only after independent probability generation. The 1D branch
provides the stronger standalone ranking signal, whereas the 3D branch contributes confidence-
weighted geometry and local chemical context. This separation makes the gain attributable to
complementary probability evidence rather than duplicated use of an earlier fused prediction.

     The 0.72 sequence weight is an empirical result of the training-OOF search, not a man-
ually imposed assertion that sequence information is intrinsically more important. For the
current canonical evaluation, both expert weights and the full-precision decision threshold
of 0.47022256531284556 were frozen from training OOF predictions and applied without
benchmark-specific reweighting, threshold adjustment, or post hoc selection between equal and
unequal fusion. Because Test1 and Test2 had been observed during earlier project development,
this statement describes the formal current protocol rather than a claim that the benchmarks
were historically blinded.
     The experts agreed on most benchmark peptides, but the discordant samples revealed a
reproducible pattern of complementary error correction: the structural expert provided correct
counter-evidence for more than one third of the sequence expert’s errors (Figure 4B). This
rescue was strongly class-asymmetric, with the structural expert mainly correcting false-positive
predictions of the sequence expert, whereas improvements in toxic-peptide recall were limited.
Peptides classified correctly only by the 3D expert also tended to be shorter, but none of the
prespecified attribute comparisons remained significant after Benjamini–Hochberg correction, so
this length pattern is descriptive rather than evidence that the structural expert is generally
superior for short peptides. These results support a complementary rather than substitutive
role for the structural expert (Figure 4).

3.3   Analysis of the Sequence and Structural Experts
     The 1D expert is not the principal innovation of this work and is analyzed only briefly.
Relative to the earlier pooled-ProtT5–TextCNN pipeline, the LoRA-adapted ProtT5 with bidi-
rectional Mamba kept BACC and MCC broadly comparable on training OOF (0.9300 vs 0.9317
and 0.8562 vs 0.8586) while improving the ranking metrics (AUROC/AUPRC 0.9761/0.9533
vs 0.9724/0.9436), and OOF-fitted Platt calibration further reduced the Brier score on Test1
and Test2 (Table 4); the stronger ranking stream benefits the subsequent fusion with structural
evidence (architecture in Section 2.3).
