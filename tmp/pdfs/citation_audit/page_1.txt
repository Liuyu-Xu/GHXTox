    GHXTox: Peptide toxicity prediction by fusing a task-adaptive
     protein language model with pLDDT-aware multi-scale 3D
                       structural modeling



                                              Abstract
          Peptide toxicity is a major safety concern in peptide-based drug development, moti-
      vating accurate computational screening methods. Existing methods have begun to ex-
      plore multi-dimensional (multimodal) feature fusion and toxicity prediction based on the
      three-dimensional (3D) conformations of peptides, yet most of them still underutilize 3D
      structural information, and systematic multi-dimensional fusion remains rare. Here, we pro-
      pose GHXTox, a hierarchical dual-level fusion framework in which a 3D structural expert
      first integrates sequence semantics with spatial structural features through predicted local-
      distance difference test (pLDDT)-aware cross-attention, and is then fused at the probability
      level with a task-adaptive one-dimensional (1D) sequence expert using weights frozen on
      training out-of-fold (OOF) predictions. The 1D branch analyzes sequences with a protein
      language model (ProtT5 fine-tuned with low-rank adaptation, LoRA) and a bidirectional
      Mamba state-space model. The 3D expert integrates an ESMFold-derived structural anchor,
      pLDDT-aware residue geometry, and full-atom-derived chemical sites. A separate residual
      head was trained on multi-conformer descriptors while the single-structure backbone was
      frozen. This residual is disabled in the canonical predictor and is reported only in con-
      trolled ablations; benchmark and public inference use the single-structure base logits. On
      the frozen historical benchmark test sets, GHXTox achieves MCC values of 0.8705 and 0.6673
      and AUPRC values of 0.9674 and 0.8046 on Test1 and Test2, respectively. Further analy-
      ses characterize residue-level sequence sensitivity, confidence–gate associations, chemical-site
      perturbations, expert disagreement, and an OOF-frozen selective-prediction rule. At real-
      ized coverages of 88.54% and 86.94%, the selective rule reduced the accepted-sample error
      to 2.71% and 3.16% on Test1 and Test2 without benchmark-specific cutoff selection. These
      findings are interpreted as model-level evidence rather than causal biological mechanisms.
      Frozen-model analyses indicate that the conformer contribution is small and non-uniform:
      under the structural expert, substantial geometric variation does not necessarily translate
      into stable outcome-aligned probability variation.

Keywords: peptide toxicity prediction; multimodal deep learning; protein language model;
bidirectional Mamba; pLDDT-aware multi-scale 3D modeling; interpretability


1     INTRODUCTION
      Peptides are increasingly used as therapeutic candidates because of their high target speci-
ficity and functional diversity [1]. However, peptide toxicity remains a major safety concern
during drug development, and the accurate and efficient identification of toxic peptides is a ma-
jor bottleneck in peptide-based drug discovery [2]. Because conventional experimental toxicity
assays are costly, time-consuming, and difficult to scale, reliable computational predictors are
highly desirable [2].
