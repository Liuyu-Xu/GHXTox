3.6                          Limitations and Future Directions
      The most fundamental limitation stems from the single-output nature of three-dimensional
conformation prediction. Short peptides in solution typically populate multiple interconvert-
ing conformations, and toxicity is usually mediated by one particular conformation or class of
conformations; a single predicted structure cannot cover this multimodal distribution. This
is precisely why the project introduced multiple conformation generators (PepFlow and Al-
phaFold2/ColabFold, assigned by length range). However, different generators exhibit inherent
noise, and normalizing conformers across models and sources further amplifies this inconsistency:
activating the multi-conformer residual on the test sets produced negligible and inconsistent ef-
fects that did not justify a public inference protocol, and source-independent and fusion-aware
candidates also failed their prespecified retention gates (Section 3.3, Tables 5 and 6), so the con-
former residual was eventually disabled for benchmark and public inference and the structural
information-extraction capacity of the model was not fully released. On training OOF the resid-
ual improved MCC by only +0.00190 (p = 0.203, not significant), and expanding the conformer
cohort from 250 to 761 peptides did not pass the retention gates because the added records were
all negative. In addition, full-atom conformational sampling (e.g., PepFlow) is computationally
far more expensive than single-structure prediction, which limited large-scale generation and
deployment. We therefore hope that next-generation protein structure prediction models can
provide, at acceptable computational cost, multiple possible conformations and their probabil-
ities (for example, lightweight multi-conformer sampling based on flow matching or diffusion),
more closely matching the multimodal nature of peptide conformation and potentially further
releasing the value of conformational information in toxicity prediction.
      The second limitation is that the 1D sequence and 3D conformation dimensions are com-
bined with a fixed 0.72/0.28 weight rather than dynamically adjusted per sample. This may
partly stem from the first limitation: the inherent noise among different conformation predic-
tors masks the information that would indicate which dimension should be trusted for a given
sample, and when structural evidence is itself inconsistent across generators, the model cannot
reliably distinguish, per sample, the reliability of sequence evidence from that of conformational
evidence and therefore falls back to fixed weights. Consistent with this, sample-level confidence
