baseline probabilities were recomputed and compared with the stored prediction vectors; the
maximum absolute discrepancy for the 1D expert within the case cohort was 3.45 × 10−4 , and
the corresponding 3D discrepancies were 5.49 × 10−4 on Test1 and 1.14 × 10−5 on Test2, none
of which changed any decision. The interpretation analyses therefore describe model sensitivity
on the frozen predictor rather than confirmed biological mechanisms.
     Mean pLDDT was strongly associated with the learned global structural gate (Spearman
0.960 on Test1, 0.950 on Test2, pooled 0.957), indicating that the model assigns less struc-
tural weight when predicted-structure confidence is lower. This is a model-use diagnostic and
is not direct evidence of peptide flexibility, disorder, or conformational heterogeneity. Across
445 individual site deletions, most changes were small (median 0.00048, mean 0.00997, 95th
percentile 0.057, maximum 0.2994 in p3D ). In the separate category-level analysis, all sites
carrying a named category were deleted together in each peptide (121 peptide–category per-
turbations). The mean absolute changes in p3D were 0.126 for hydrophobic-category deletion,
0.0687 for aromatic-category deletion, and 0.00179 for sulfur-category deletion. These category-
level sensitivities do not describe individual-site effects and should not be interpreted as causal
toxicophores. The opposing directions of the largest deletion effects (Phe8 increased p3D , Pro19
decreased it) show that deletion scores depend on the surrounding peptide context and cannot
be read as an intrinsic toxicity label for a functional group (Figure 7).
     Fixed late fusion agreed on most benchmark peptides, and the discordant cases illustrate its
operational consequences (the aggregate pattern is reported in Section 3.2). The toxic peptide
sequence_23_23 (ACLDIGNSCRED, two cysteines) had a weak 1D probability of 0.2806 but
a high-confidence 3D probability of 0.9954; fusion raised the probability to 0.4808 and restored
the correct toxic classification. At the model level, this example shows that a cysteine-associated
structural pattern supplied counter-evidence to a weak 1D score; it does not establish a cysteine-
mediated biological mechanism. The toxic peptide sequence_34_34 had a correct 1D probability
of 0.5447 but a 3D probability of 3×10−5 , and fusion reduced the probability to 0.3922, producing
a false negative; strong sequence evidence can therefore be overridden when the structural expert
supplies sufficiently strong opposing evidence, and such single-sample evidence conflicts warrant
further inspection (Figure 8). These cases illustrate the operational premise and failure modes
of the model: its 1D and 3D scores can agree or conflict, and fixed late fusion can either correct
or introduce an error. They do not confirm a causal biological role for any particular structural
feature.
