masked before the selective scan and excluded from masked pooling; because the depthwise con-
volution precedes this scan mask in the implementation, the reported inference protocol retains
the canonical batch construction. The resulting residue states are aggregated by masked mean
pooling over the valid positions, and a layer-normalized single-output linear head yields the un-
calibrated toxicity probability. Because non-toxic peptides far outnumber toxic peptides in the
training data, a positive-class-weighted binary cross-entropy loss is used instead of SMOTE or
Borderline-SMOTE, with the positive-class weight w+ = N− /N+ recomputed from each fold’s
training partition; this emphasizes the minority class without generating synthetic samples or
leaking held-out information.
      To reduce the variance of a single training run, three 1D models are trained under identi-
cal settings with random seeds 42, 123, and 2025, and the ensemble score is the equal-weight
arithmetic average of the three full-data models; no seed is selected on test performance and no
seed-specific weight is introduced. Although averaging reduces variance, neural-network proba-
bilities may remain miscalibrated, so a monotonic two-parameter Platt calibrator [24] is fitted
to the training OOF scores (generated with the fixed group-aware five-fold partition, Section
2.5) and applied to the three-seed average at inference. Because the calibration is monotonic, it
preserves the sample ranking. Test1/Test2 labels are not used for member selection, calibration
fitting, or threshold optimization. The calibrated probability p1D is the sequence-expert output
used in the top-level fusion (Section 2.5); no additional calibrator is fitted after fusion.

2.4   pLDDT-Aware Multi-Scale 3D Structural Modeling
     Peptide toxicity is influenced not only by amino acid composition and linear residue order
but also by the three-dimensional (3D) arrangement of charged groups, hydrophobic regions,
aromatic systems, and sulfur-containing moieties. Because peptides are generally short, con-
formationally flexible, and poorly covered by experimentally determined structures, treating
a single predicted conformation as an error-free structural observation may amplify structure-
prediction errors, whereas Cα-only or peptide-level summary representations fail to capture the
local spatial-chemical environments that are relevant to toxicity [6–8].
     To address these limitations, GHXTox employs a pLDDT-aware multi-scale structural ex-
pert that captures complementary single-structure information at four levels: residue seman-
tics, spatial residue neighborhoods, full-atom chemical sites, and peptide-level global proper-
ties. Operationally, ESMFold provides a full-atom structural anchor and residue-level pLDDT
values, while frozen ESM2 embeddings and explicit residue descriptors provide the internal
sequence-semantic states. Cα geometry is encoded on a hybrid sequence/spatial-neighbor graph
by a pLDDT-weighted equivariant network, and full-atom chemical pseudo-sites provide a zero-
initialized residue-level correction. Contextual sequence states then query the confidence-gated


                                                   5
spatial states through cross-attention; masked residue pooling and peptide-level physicochemical
descriptors produce the structural logit. A separately trained, availability-masked conformer-
ensemble residual can modify this logit only in controlled ablations and is disabled in the canon-
ical benchmark and public-inference pathway.
     Sections 2.4.1–2.4.6 specify this pipeline in order: single-structure prediction and conformer
preparation, residue feature construction and the hybrid geometric graph, pLDDT-aware equiv-
ariant encoding, full-atom chemical-site message passing and the optional conformer residual,
sequence–structure cross-attention, and global feature integration with classification. Through-
out, pLDDT is treated as a continuous reliability signal rather than a hard filter. It enters the
residue and edge features, weights geometric and chemical-site messages, and controls residue-
and peptide-level structural gates. Low-confidence structural evidence is thereby down-weighted
rather than removed, preserving structural coverage while limiting the influence of uncertain co-
ordinates. pLDDT is interpreted here as a local confidence estimate produced by the structure-
prediction model, not as an experimentally determined measure of structural accuracy, confor-
mational flexibility, or biological activity.

2.4.1   Peptide Structure Prediction and Conformer Preparation
     For a peptide sequence of length L, S = (a1 , a2 , . . . , aL ), ESMFold is first used to pre-
dict its three-dimensional structure [12]. The predicted structure is stored as a full-atom
PDB file, from which residue-level Cα coordinates, backbone coordinates, full-atom-derived
chemical-site coordinates, and pLDDT scores are extracted. The Cα coordinates are collected
as X = [x1 , . . . , xL ]⊤ ∈ RL×3 , A denotes the full-atom-PDB-derived backbone and chemical-site
geometry, and q = (q1 , . . . , qL ) ∈ [0, 1]L contains the residue-level pLDDT confidence scores
rescaled to [0, 1], where qi denotes the residue-level pLDDT score of residue i. The Cα coordi-
nates serve as the primary spatial variables of the residue-level equivariant graph, whereas the
full-atom coordinates are used to construct local chemical sites. Before geometric encoding, the
coordinates are centered at the peptide centroid to remove the effect of absolute translation.
     A single predicted structure provides a common structural anchor but cannot represent
potential conformational heterogeneity. The conformer-residual stage was therefore trained,
with the single-structure spatial backbone, chemical-site branch, and base classifier frozen, on a
multi-source conformer variant using a 2,795-peptide cohort with up to eight validated, aligned
conformers per peptide. PepFlow is a generalized Boltzmann generator that performs direct
all-atom conformational-ensemble sampling from the peptide energy landscape rather than pre-
dicting a single structure; it has been shown to efficiently recapitulate experimental ensembles
of short linear motifs (SLiMs, typically 3–15 residues) and to generate all-atom conformations
for peptides of different lengths [15]. Short-peptide conformations are therefore provided by
PepFlow. In our pipeline, up to eight conformations are retained per input peptide, and
PepFlow is applied to peptides of at most 15 residues (the actual training cohort spans 11–
15 residues and comprises 761 records with eight conformers each). Longer peptides are pro-
vided by AlphaFold2/ColabFold: AlphaFold2 is a high-accuracy single-structure predictor [13],
and its peptide-structure performance has been systematically evaluated for peptides of 16–60
residues [8], an interval that covers the 16–30-residue cohort; ColabFold, an MSA-accelerated
AlphaFold2 implementation [14], is run in our pipeline with five models and multiple seeds to
generate candidate conformations. These candidates are filtered by an average-pLDDT quality
floor and then reduced to eight diverse conformers per peptide by farthest-point sampling on
Kabsch-aligned Cα-RMSD [25], yielding 2,034 training records. These sources are represented
explicitly in the residual head; nevertheless, generator identity remains partially coupled to
peptide length and is treated as a limitation rather than as an independently identified causal
factor.
     For controlled ablation only, a test-side conformer cache was constructed with fixed source
and sequence-matching policies without using Test1 or Test2 labels; these caches are not used

quality gating, which remains governed by the ESMFold backbone, and PepFlow contributes
only cross-conformer fluctuation and occupancy information. The normalized ensemble statis-
tics are consumed by a small logit residual branch controlled by a binary availability indicator
cconf (mechanism detailed in Section 2.4.4); when cconf = 0, the correction vanishes exactly and
the model reduces to the single-structure pathway. Missing conformer statistics are treated as
unavailable data rather than as evidence of structural rigidity; the model draws no flexibility
inference from the absence of conformers and simply falls back to the single-structure prediction.
     The generated conformers are computational samples rather than experimentally deter-
mined trajectories or rigorous thermodynamic ensembles. Consequently, RMSF, contact occu-
pancy, and distance variance are interpreted as sampling-derived descriptors of conformational
variation rather than direct measurements of peptide dynamics in solution.

2.4.2   Residue Feature Construction and Hybrid Geometric Graph
     Given the predicted single structure and its confidence scores (Section 2.4.1), the struc-
tural expert constructs two complementary residue-level representations: an internal sequence-
semantic representation and a spatial-geometric representation. The sequence-semantic sub-
branch uses frozen ESM2 residue embeddings [12] and a lightweight Transformer; unlike the
independent top-level 1D prediction expert of Section 2.3, it serves the residue-level sequence–
structure alignment inside the structural expert.
