Abstract. As fundamental signaling molecules, neuropeptides are involved in diverse
physiological regulatory activities. Abnormal expression patterns have been closely
linked to neurological and metabolic disorders, which underscores the urgent need for
precise and rapid identification methods. Here, we develop NeuroPpred-PSCG, a novel
multimodal deep learning framework. At its core, the framework employs both a
bidirectional cross-attention mechanism and a gated fusion module to integrate global
semantic insights from the protein language model (ProtT5) with local conformational
data derived from secondary structure. Furthermore, it utilizes a multi-scale gated
convolutional network to enhance hierarchical contextual information. On the
independent test set, NeuroPpred-PSCG surpasses current leading methods on most
metrics, achieving ACC of 94.8%, SN of 95.1%, F1-score of 94.8%, and MCC of 0.896,
reflecting robust overall discriminative capability. Compared to the previous best-
performing model, NeuroPpred-MSN, our method shows improvements of 1.2% and
0.024 in ACC and MCC, respectively, and most notably, a significant 2.8% gain in SN.
Ablation studies, robustness analysis, and interpretability analysis further substantiate
the effectiveness of each component, as well as the overall stability and interpretability
of NeuroPpred-PSCG. The source code and datasets have been made publicly
accessible at https://github.com/xinyizhang186/NeuroPpred-PSCG.

1 INTRODUCTION
  Neuropeptides are small, bioactive molecules secreted by the nervous system,
typically ranging from 3 to 100 amino acids in length [1, 2]. As critical signaling
mediators, neuropeptides are involved in a wide array of physiological processes,
including learning and memory, mood regulation, sleep-wake cycles, and energy
metabolism [3, 4]. Neuropeptide dysregulation is implicated in a spectrum of
neurological and metabolic disorders, such as Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease,
depression, and obesity [5-7]. However, traditional homology-based methods and deep
learning models designed for other generic bioactive peptides may not generalize well
to neuropeptide identification due to their high sequence heterogeneity, variable length,
and complex post-translational modifications [8-10]. Therefore, developing reliable
computational methods for efficient, large-scale discovery of neuropeptides is
imperative.
  A cornerstone of neuropeptide prediction has been the use of traditional machine
learning. This approach centers on pairing classical classifiers with manually
engineered features. For instance, an early predictor, NeuroPIpred, utilized a support
vector machine based on four classes of physicochemical features [11]. Subsequent
work has developed along two principal directions. One path aims to construct more
sophisticated models through strategies such as stacking and ensemble learning, as
illustrated by PredNeuroP’s dual-layer framework [12]. The other path focuses on
optimizing the input data via feature selection, a technique utilized by NeuroPpred-Fuse
and NeuroPred-FRL with their single-step and two-step strategies, respectively [13, 14].
Despite their straightforward nature, these features can only produce shallow sequence
representations, thereby failing to capture the deep contextual information required for
accurate prediction.
  The paradigm for neuropeptide prediction has recently shifted towards deep learning.
Liu et al. spearheaded this transition with an early breakthrough, NeuroCNN_GNB.
Their model, which integrated a convolutional neural network with Gaussian Naive
Bayes, was significant for proving the viability of automatic feature extraction [15].
With the rise of pre-trained protein language models (PLMs), including ESM, BERT,
and ProtTrans, further progress has been achieved in this field. These approaches
extract informative latent features from protein and peptide sequences and have been
successfully applied to diverse proteomics and peptide-related prediction tasks [16, 17].
Specifically, although NeuroPred-PLM [18] combined ESM embeddings with a multi-
scale convolutional network, its simplistic feature aggregation scheme limited its ability
to capture deep sequential dependencies. Likewise, NeuroPred-SVM [19] employed a
shallow classifier that struggled to fully exploit hierarchical interactions from BERT
embeddings. To enrich representations, fusion strategies like NeuroPred-AIMP [20]
and NeuroPpred-SHE [21] integrated heterogeneous descriptors; however, they
remained limited by simple feature concatenation, which often introduced redundancy
and noise without adaptively selecting optimal signals. Furthermore, even the recent
state-of-the-art   framework,    NeuroPpred-MSN        [22],   which    aligned   diverse
representations via a Siamese network and contrastive learning, still lacked an explicit
gated filtering mechanism. Collectively, these architectural bottlenecks underscore the
necessity for a framework that synergizes deep multi-scale context extraction with
adaptive feature purification.
  To overcome the reliance of existing methods on unimodal feature representations
and to mitigate the issue of information redundancy in multi-feature fusion, we propose
NeuroPpred-PSCG, a novel multimodal deep learning framework. The core of this
framework is the sophisticated integration of two complementary feature types: (1)
Global semantic information, which encodes relationships between distant amino acids
and evolutionary context using a pre-trained protein language model (ProtT5); (2) Local
conformational features, which represent the local spatial arrangement of the sequence
via predicted secondary structure information. To ensure the effective fusion of this
heterogeneous information, we design a two-stage integration module. First, a
bidirectional cross-attention mechanism facilitates deep interaction and alignment
between the two feature modalities. Subsequently, a gated fusion mechanism adaptively
assigns weights to the features, which allows for the dynamic prioritization of
information most crucial for prediction. On this basis, a multi-scale gated convolutional
network is applied to extract contextual patterns from the fused features, thereby
boosting their expressive capacity. Finally, an attention pooling layer aggregates
residue-level features into a sequence-level representation for classification.
NeuroPpred-PSCG achieves competitive performance on the independent test set.
Furthermore, extensive ablation studies, robustness analyses, and interpretability
analyses confirm the model’s effective discriminative ability and its potential for
practical applications.
2 MATERIALS AND METHODS

2.1 Datasets
  The study employs the public benchmark dataset previously established by Wang et
al. to ensure a fair and comparable assessment [18]. In their data construction pipeline,
the positive samples consisted of 11,282 experimentally confirmed neuropeptides
collected from the NeuroPep 2.0 database [23]. These sequences underwent a two-stage
filtering process: first, sequences of length 5 to 100 amino acids were retained;
subsequently, the CD-HIT tool (version 4.8.1) was utilized to eliminate redundancy at
a 90% sequence identity cutoff [24], resulting in 4463 non-redundant neuropeptide
sequences. For dataset balancing, negative samples were randomly extracted by Wang
et al. from the UniProt database and then processed using the identical procedure to
ensure matching length distributions and redundancy removal. In total, the dataset
contains 8926 sequences and was divided into training and independent testing subsets
at a 9:1 split, which we strictly adhere to in our evaluation to ensure an unbiased and
standard comparison.
  Additionally, to evaluate the model’s generalization under class-imbalanced
conditions, we employ two imbalanced datasets from Wen et al. [21]. These datasets
originate from the same source as the benchmark data but were constructed by the
original authors using a more stringent 0.3 similarity cutoff for deduplication, yielding
454 positive samples and 2,542 negative samples (a ratio of approximately 1:5.6). This
collection was then partitioned at a 9:1 ratio to form a training dataset and an
imbalanced testing dataset. The external independent test set was specifically compiled
from neuropeptides published after 2023 in the NCBI and PDB databases. Processed
via the same pipeline, it contains 115 positive and 644 negative samples. Models are
trained on the imbalanced training set and evaluated on both independent test sets. All
datasets are summarized in Table 1.

2.2 Overview of the Model
  As illustrated in Figure 1, NeuroPpred-PSCG employs a multimodal deep learning
framework to achieve accurate neuropeptide identification. The model’s input layer
processes two heterogeneous feature modalities in parallel. The first modality utilizes
the pre-trained protein language model ProtT5 to capture global semantic
representations and latent evolutionary patterns from the sequence [25]. The second
integrates local conformational features derived from the secondary structure, as
predicted by the PHAT web server [26]. Subsequently, these two modalities undergo
deep interaction and alignment through a bidirectional cross-attention mechanism. This
is followed by a linear gated fusion unit that performs adaptive weighting to emphasize
the most discriminative information. Based on the fused features, a multi-scale gated
convolutional network is introduced to model hierarchical contextual dependencies,
thereby enhancing their expressive capacity. Finally, an attention pooling layer
aggregates sequence-level features for the final prediction.
2.2.1 ProtT5 Semantic Embedding
  To capture context-aware features from neuropeptide sequences, we utilize the pre-
trained protein language model ProtT5-XL-UniRef50 (ProtT5) [25]. The model
contains 33 Transformer layers and is pre-trained on 650 million UniRef50 protein
sequences via a masked language modeling objective. Through this pre-training, the
model can implicitly learn evolutionary information and the relationships between
sequence, structure, and function. In practice, for any given amino acid sequence S,
ProtT5 generates a 1024-dimensional embedding for each residue. To align these raw
embeddings for the cross-modal interaction module, we employ a linear layer to map
them into a shared 320-dimensional latent space, based on the hyperparameter
optimization described below. This procedure significantly reduces computational costs
while accentuating the semantic information pertinent to our prediction task.
2.2.2 Secondary Structure Embedding
  For this work, we employ the PHAT web server to predict the secondary structure of
neuropeptide sequences [26]. The PHAT model integrates a powerful pre-trained
protein language model with a hypergraph-based multi-head attention architecture,
which effectively transfers semantic information from large proteins to peptide
sequences. It can generate predictions in both three-state (H: alpha-helix, E: extended
beta-strand, C: coil/others) and eight-state (H: alpha-helix, G: 310-helix, I: π-helix, E:
extended beta-strand, B: isolated beta-strand, T: turn, S: bend, C: coil/others) formats
[27, 28]. For each generated secondary structure sequence, we first convert it into an
initial structural vector using token and positional embedding techniques. Subsequently,
a linear projection layer maps this structural vector to a dimensional space consistent
with that of the reduced-dimension ProtT5 embeddings. This step performs feature-
dimension alignment before cross-modal fusion and yields the final secondary structure
representation used by the gated cross-attention module.
2.2.3 Gated Cross-Attention Mechanism
  NeuroPpred-PSCG introduces a gated cross-attention mechanism (Figure 1) to foster
deep integration between ProtT5 semantic embeddings and secondary structure features
[29]. Unlike simple feature addition or concatenation, this mechanism leverages both
bidirectional cross-attention and a linear gate to enable reciprocal enhancement.
Specifically, deep semantic information from ProtT5 embeddings strengthens the
secondary structure characterization, while local conformational details from the
secondary structure, in turn, provide the embeddings with rich structural information.
The features are then passed through the linear gating unit, which dynamically adjusts
and combines the information flow from the two modalities for efficient and adaptable
fusion.
(1) Cross-Attention Mechanism
  Cross-attention extends the standard attention mechanism, specifically designed to
model the interdependencies between two heterogeneous feature sequences [30]. It
enables one feature sequence, acting as the Query, to dynamically aggregate relevant
information from another sequence, which serves as the Key and Value, thereby
facilitating cross-modal alignment. Specifically, the bidirectional interaction is driven
by two explicit information flows. In the ProtT5-to-ss8 flow, ss8 features act as the
Query, and ProtT5 embeddings serve as the Key and Value, enabling structural features
to integrate global semantic context. Conversely, in the ss8-to-ProtT5 flow, ProtT5
embeddings query ss8 features to incorporate local structural cues. These dual pathways
facilitate effective alignment between semantic and structural representations. The
cross-attention operation follows Equations (1) and (2).

the gradient direction of the loss function, which serve to examine the model’s
sensitivity to minor input alterations.

3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

3.1 Performance Comparison with Existing Models
  This study compares NeuroPpred-PSCG with eight representative neuropeptide
prediction methods using the unified independent test set. For consistency and
transparency, the baseline results are taken directly from the original publications,
which used the same benchmark datasets and train/test partitions [18, 21]. This avoids
potential bias from unofficial reimplementations or suboptimal hyperparameter tuning.
As shown in Table 2, NeuroPpred-PSCG outperforms competing methods across
multiple metrics. Specifically, it achieves the highest scores in ACC, SN, F1-score, and
MCC, reaching 94.8%, 95.1%, 94.8%, and 0.896, respectively. Relative to the current
state-of-the-art model NeuroPpred-MSN, NeuroPpred-PSCG improves ACC and MCC
by 1.2% and 0.024, respectively, reflecting stronger discriminative capability. It is
worth noting that NeuroPred-FRL reports the highest precision (96.0%), but its
sensitivity remains limited (75.7%), implying a higher rate of missed positives. In
contrast, NeuroPpred-PSCG achieves the highest sensitivity (95.1%) while maintaining
a competitive precision (94.6%), resulting in a more balanced trade-off between
detecting positive samples and controlling false positives. Furthermore, NeuroPpred-
PSCG attains an AUROC of 0.980, which is comparable to the best value reported by
NeuroPred-MSN (0.983). This suggests that the model maintains consistent
performance across varying classification thresholds. To validate the performance
differences, we apply the Friedman test and pairwise Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. The
Friedman test reveals significant overall differences among the models (p < 0.001).
Subsequent pairwise analysis demonstrates that NeuroPpred-PSCG significantly
outperforms NeuroPred-FRL (p = 0.0313) and shows a marginally significant
improvement over NeuroPpred-MSN at the 0.10 level (p = 0.0781). For the remaining
six methods, our model demonstrates significant superiority (all p = 0.0156). Together,
these results provide statistical support for the superior performance of our model.

  To refine the architecture of NeuroPpred-PSCG, we perform a systematic
hyperparameter analysis covering both the choice of pre-trained backbone and key
architectural settings. The initial investigation focuses on four prominent protein
language models: ESM-2, ProtT5, ProtBERT, and ProtALBERT [25, 35]错误!未找到
引用源。. As illustrated in Figure 2, ProtT5 consistently achieves the best performance
across all metrics, indicating its strong capability in capturing informative sequence
representations. ESM-2 ranks second, with ACC of 93.9% and MCC of 0.878. In
contrast, ProtBERT and ProtALBERT show inferior performance, with ProtBERT
exhibiting the most noticeable decline (a reduction of 3.5% in ACC and 0.069 in MCC
relative to ProtT5). These results underscore the importance of ProtT5 for downstream
prediction tasks.
   Using ProtT5 as the backbone, we further tune key architectural hyperparameters.
Table 3 shows that the projection dimension of 320 achieves the strongest overall
performance (ACC: 94.8%, F1-score: 94.8%, MCC: 0.896). Increasing the dimension
to 384 slightly improves sensitivity (95.7%) but negatively affects overall performance,
while a larger dimension (512) leads to an additional drop. This suggests that moderate
dimensionality better balances representation capacity and redundancy. For the multi-
scale gated CNN, the kernel configuration (3, 5) yields optimal results, while larger
combinations, including (5, 7) and (7, 9), reduce overall performance. We also examine
the number of attention heads h in the gated cross-attention module. Among h = 2, 4, 8,
and 16, h = 8 achieves the best overall performance. Reducing h to 2 decreases ACC
and MCC to 93.6% and 0.872, respectively, whereas increasing h to 16 reduces these
values to 94.0% and 0.881. Overall, backbone choice and architectural tuning jointly
dictate performance.
3.3 Ablation Experiments
  A series of ablation studies is performed to systematically assess the contributions of
the key modules within NeuroPpred-PSCG. We benchmark the performance of multiple
model variants against the complete model (our baseline) on the independent test set,
which allows us to verify the efficacy of the multimodal features and the cross-modal
fusion mechanism.
3.3.1 Analysis of the Effectiveness of Multimodal Features
  To investigate the contributions of each feature modality, we design three model
variants: (i) one using only 3-state secondary structure information (designated as w/o
ProtT5, w/ss3); (ii) one using only 8-state secondary structure information (w/o ProtT5,
w/ss8); and (iii) one using only ProtT5 semantic features (w/o ss8, w/ProtT5). As
illustrated in Figure 3A and Figure 3C, the model’s performance degrades substantially
upon the removal of ProtT5 features when relying solely on either ss3 or ss8
information. Specifically, the ACC drops to 72.3% and 72.6%, the MCC to 0.449 and
0.453, and the AUROC to 0.756 and 0.791, respectively. This indicates that structural
information alone is insufficient to adequately capture the high-level semantic context
of neuropeptide sequences. Conversely, when retaining only the ProtT5 semantic
features, the model’s performance recovers markedly, achieving an ACC of 94.3% and
an MCC of 0.885. This finding highlights the importance of the pre-trained protein
language model in capturing informative sequence patterns. Finally, the full model
demonstrates superior performance for all indicators, providing strong evidence for the
significant complementarity and synergy between semantic and structural information.
  To further evaluate the cost-effectiveness of introducing ss8-derived structural
information, we analyze model size and computational cost. Model size is characterized
by trainable parameters and FP32 memory footprint, while computational cost is
measured by MACs and FLOPs per sample. As shown in


  Table 4, compared to the ProtT5-only variant (w/o ss8), the full NeuroPpred-PSCG
model introduces a moderate increase in parameters (from 1.189 M to 1.827 M) and
computational cost (from 0.249 G to 0.388 G FLOPs/sample). Importantly, this
bounded overhead yields consistent performance gains, improving ACC from 94.3% to
94.8% and MCC from 0.885 to 0.896. As detailed in Table 5, the total overhead of
integrating the ss8 modality includes 0.639 M parameters, 2.555 MB of FP32 storage,
and 0.140 G FLOPs/sample. This additional cost stems primarily from the cross-
attention and gated fusion modules. In contrast, the ss8 embedding itself is remarkably
lightweight, requiring only 0.022 M parameters and 0.004 G FLOPs/sample. Although
ProtT5 remains the primary predictive source, the performance gains from structural
cues justify this marginal complexity, confirming the efficiency of our multimodal
architecture.
3.3.2 Contribution Analysis of the Cross-Modal Fusion Mechanism
  To investigate the necessity of the cross-modal fusion mechanism, we construct the
following variants: (i) a model with both the cross-attention and the gated convolutional
network removed (designated as w/o crossatt, w/o gatecnn); (ii) one retaining only the
cross-attention module (w/o gatecnn, w/ crossatt); and (iii) one retaining only the gated
convolutional network (w/o crossatt, w/ gatecnn). As shown in Figure 3B, completely
removing the fusion module leads to a significant performance drop, with the ACC
decreasing from 94.8% to 93.2% and the MCC from 0.896 to 0.865. Further comparison
reveals that the model with only cross-attention (ACC: 94.4%, MCC: 0.888) slightly
outperforms the one with only the gated convolutional (ACC: 94.3%, MCC: 0.885),
although both are inferior to the full NeuroPpred-PSCG model. This highlights the
necessity of the gated convolutional, whose adaptive filtering mechanism effectively
suppresses noise and refines the fused features, complementing the cross-modal
interactions established by the attention module. The coordinated interaction of these
two modules is essential for improving feature fusion, which in turn supports the
underlying design of the NeuroPpred-PSCG architecture.

3.4 Evaluation of Model Robustness and Generalization
3.4.1 Robustness Analysis Under Embedding and Sequence-Level Perturbations
  We analyze the robustness of NeuroPpred-PSCG using three rigorous tests: 10-fold
cross-validation, FGM-based adversarial testing, and BLOSUM62-guided conservative
substitutions. The cross-validation results (Table 6) indicate stable performance across
different data splits, with ACC ranging from 93.4% to 94.9%. The average scores are
93.89% ± 0.44 for ACC, 93.90% ± 0.43 for F1-score, and 0.879 ± 0.009 for MCC. A
high AUROC of 0.971 ± 0.015, together with small standard deviations, further
indicates that the model is not sensitive to data partitioning. Under FGM perturbations
(Table 7), the model performance remains largely unaffected. Only minor reductions
are observed in ACC (94.8% to 94.5%), MCC (0.896 to 0.890), and AUROC (0.980 to
0.973), confirming the model’s robustness against adversarial noise. This indicates that
NeuroPpred-PSCG remains stable under data variability and input perturbations.
  Subsequently, we evaluate sequence-level robustness on the independent test set
using BLOSUM62-guided conservative amino acid substitutions. Specifically, 1%, 5%,
and 10% of residues are randomly replaced with biochemically similar amino acids
(BLOSUM62 scores ≥ 0), and each setting is repeated five times using fixed random
seeds. For each mutated sequence set, ProtT5 and ss8 features are regenerated before
prediction. Conservative substitutions are used because they are more likely to preserve
the biochemical properties of short neuropeptides than insertions or deletions, which
may alter peptide length, sequence motifs, and biological activity. As shown in


  Table 8, NeuroPpred-PSCG maintains stable performance under 1%, 5%, and 10%
conservative substitutions, with ACC values of 92.06%, 92.34%, and 91.22%,
respectively, AUROC values above 0.967, and prediction consistency above 94.28%.
Table 9 further shows that mutation sensitivity analysis yields small mean absolute
probability shifts (0.041–0.053) and limited mean confidence drops (<0.046),
suggesting that NeuroPpred-PSCG is reasonably stable under mild, biochemically
constrained sequence-level variations.
3.4.2 Analysis of the Model's Generalization Ability on the Imbalanced Test Sets
  To assess the generalization capability of NeuroPpred-PSCG on imbalanced datasets,
we train and test our model on the dataset constructed by Wen et al. [21]. As detailed in
Table 10 and Table 11, baseline metrics are directly adopted from their original
publications. On the imbalanced independent test set, NeuroPpred-PSCG achieves
strong overall performance in terms of ACC (96.0%), F1-score (87.0%), and MCC
(0.846). Although PredNeuroP achieved the highest ACC (99.0%), its much lower SN
(64.4%) indicates a bias toward the majority class. However, NeuroPpred-PSCG strikes
a superior balance between SN (88.9%) and SP (97.2%). Although NeuroPpred-MSN
reported a slightly higher AUROC (0.974), its lower MCC and F1-score suggest that its
overall discriminative power and stability are inferior to our model. The Friedman test
further confirms a significant overall difference among models (p = 0.0015). At a
significance level of 0.1, Wilcoxon tests indicate that NeuroPpred-PSCG significantly
outperforms NeuroPpred-SHE, NeuroPpred-Fuse, NeuroPred-FRL, and PredNeuroP,
with p-values of 0.0547, 0.0234, 0.0156, and 0.0391, respectively. The comparisons
with NeuroPpred-MSN and NeuroPred-PLM are not statistically significant (both p =
0.1484); however, the metric-level results indicate that NeuroPpred-PSCG achieves a
better trade-off between positive-sample detection and negative-sample discrimination.
  On the external imbalanced test set, NeuroPpred-PSCG also achieves the highest
ACC (88.8%) and MCC (0.493), showcasing its robust, comprehensive discriminative
ability. Although its F1-score is marginally lower than that of NeuroPpred-SHE (55.0%),
NeuroPpred-PSCG maintains a lead in precision (73.4%) and specificity (97.4%),
indicating a significant advantage in controlling false positives. Taken together, these
results demonstrate that NeuroPpred-PSCG achieves a superior performance trade-off
and greater robustness in highly imbalanced data scenarios. Similarly, the Friedman test
reveals a significant overall performance difference among the models (p < 0.001). The
post-hoc Wilcoxon tests show that NeuroPpred-PSCG significantly outperforms
NeuroPred-PLM, NeuroPpred-Fuse, NeuroPred-FRL, and PredNeuroP, with all
comparisons yielding p = 0.0078. The comparisons with NeuroPpred-MSN and
NeuroPpred-SHE are not statistically significant, with p-values of 0.4688 and 0.3281,
respectively. Nevertheless, NeuroPpred-PSCG achieves the best ACC, SP, Pre, and
MCC, supporting its advantage in overall discrimination and false-positive control.

3.6.1 Feature Importance Analysis
  To interpret the model’s predictions, we evaluate the contributions of ProtT5 and ss8
features using SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analysis 错误!未找到引用源。.
As shown in Figure 5 and Figure 6, the top 10 most influential features all originate
from ProtT5 embeddings. The dominance of ProtT5 is further evidenced by its mean
absolute SHAP value (0.001412), which is significantly higher than that of the ss8
features (0.000199), demonstrating its leading role in driving model predictions at the
global level. At the feature level, the SHAP distributions also reveal distinct directional
effects. For instance, lower values (blue) in ProtT5_144, ProtT5_288, and ProtT5_84
drive positive predictions, whereas higher values (red) in ProtT5_277, ProtT5_54, and
ProtT5_69 favor positive outcomes. Furthermore, the asymmetric and scattered
distribution of these SHAP values implies that the model relies on non-linear, context-
dependent interactions rather than simple monotonic trends. These results validate the
strong discriminative capacity of ProtT5 embeddings for neuropeptide identification.
3.6.2 Attention-Based Interpretability Analysis
  To investigate the internal decision-making mechanism of the NeuroPpred-PSCG
model and evaluate its interpretability, we visualize its cross-attention weights and
compare them with conserved motifs identified by MEME [38]. As illustrated in Figure
7, the attention weights are highly concentrated on the “FQGMR” region, and the high-
attention region not only aligns precisely with the “WFGPR” motif identified by
MEME but also significantly extends to cover the upstream “PGL” segment. This
strong concordance between the key sequences autonomously learned by the model and
known conserved motifs provides compelling evidence that NeuroPpred-PSCG not
only exhibits excellent classification performance but can also accurately identify the
critical sequence patterns that drive its decisions. The attention mechanism reveals the
intrinsic rationale and biological basis of the model’s predictions by assigning greater
weights to the amino acid residues with higher contributions.
3.6.3 Mutation-Scan Analysis
  To investigate residue-level interpretability, we perform in silico mutagenesis (ISM)
on the independent test set. Each residue position is systematically substituted with the
20 standard amino acids, and the resulting mutant sequences are re-predicted. As shown
in Figure 8A and Figure 8B, the sequence logos are generated using the kpLogo web
server [39]. Positive samples are enriched with residues such as S, L, R, and G, whereas
negative samples show a clear enrichment of methionine (M) at the N-terminal position.
The ISM heatmaps of three representative neuropeptides further demonstrate how these
residue preferences influence model predictions (Figure 8C-E). Substituting the N-
terminal residue with M markedly reduces the prediction score, consistent with its
enrichment in non-neuropeptides. Similarly, substitutions to cysteine (C), particularly
at internal positions, lead to low-score regions, agreeing with the low frequency of C in
positive neuropeptide logos. To reduce sequence-specific bias, all positive samples are
normalized into 10 position-ratio intervals, and a global ISM visualization is generated
(Figure 8F). This analysis confirms that M exerts its strongest effect at the first position-
ratio interval, while C shows strong effects across multiple internal-to-C-terminal
intervals. Together, these results indicate that NeuroPpred-PSCG captures biologically
meaningful positional residue preferences rather than relying on simple sequence
memorization.

3.7 Limitations and Future Directions
  Despite NeuroPpred-PSCG’s competitive performance, neuropeptide identification
remains more challenging than predicting other short bioactive peptides. Although
prediction models developed for antiviral peptides (e.g., TargetAVP-DeepCaps [40]),
antimicrobial peptides (e.g., deep-AMPpred [41]), and anticancer peptides (e.g., pACP-
HybDeep [42]) often report higher evaluation metrics, these methods cannot be directly
transferred to neuropeptide identification. Neuropeptides typically exhibit high
sequence     heterogeneity,    weak     evolutionary     conservation,     limited   shared
physicochemical motifs, distinct functional mechanisms, and complex post-
translational processing. These factors complicate identification and suggest that cross-
task performance comparisons should be interpreted cautiously. Given these challenges,
NeuroPpred-PSCG’s balanced performance confirms the effectiveness of integrating
ProtT5-derived semantics with structural information via adaptive cross-modal fusion.
  Nevertheless, several limitations remain and should be addressed in future work. Our
future efforts will focus on the following four aspects: (1) Integration of 3D structural
information: The current model is built upon sequence information and secondary
structure predictions, without explicitly modeling the 3D conformations that are closely
linked to biological function. Introducing structural data predicted by AlphaFold2 [43]
or ESMFold [44] may help capture spatial arrangements and functional domains more
effectively, which could contribute to improved prediction accuracy. (2) Generalization
under external imbalanced conditions: While NeuroPpred-PSCG maintains competitive
overall performance on the external imbalanced test set, its sensitivity drops under
external distribution shifts. This suggests that identifying positive neuropeptides
remains challenging despite comparable class ratios, possibly due to variations in
sequence composition and data sources. Future work will explore threshold calibration
and cost-sensitive learning to enhance sensitivity and real-world robustness. (3)
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT): The present study relies on static ProtT5
embeddings, without further adapting the pre-trained model to the target task. Applying
PEFT methods, such as LoRA [45], could enable efficient fine-tuning with a limited
number of additional parameters. This could enhance its ability to capture task-relevant
patterns in neuropeptide sequences, while preserving efficiency in computation and
memory usage. (4) Generalization to a wider range of bioactive peptides: NeuroPpred-
PSCG has demonstrated considerable versatility. We plan to extend to other bioactive
peptide tasks, such as antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory peptide prediction, to
explore its potential as a general-purpose framework.

4 CONCLUSIONS
  This study introduces NeuroPpred-PSCG, a multimodal deep learning framework for
neuropeptide identification. The model architecture integrates information across
different scales. Through the application of a bidirectional cross-attention mechanism
and a multi-scale gated convolutional network, it harmonizes the high-level semantic
insights captured by the ProtT5 language model with the localized structural patterns
defined by secondary structures, thus facilitating a hierarchical analysis of the sequence.
Our method achieves consistent improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods
in overall performance. Moreover, ablation results highlight the importance of the
multimodal fusion strategy for model performance. Additional evaluations of
robustness and generalization, together with visualization analyses, show that the model
remains stable and captures biologically meaningful patterns. Overall, this study
highlights the potential of multimodal deep fusion in characterizing complex biological
sequences, offering a promising approach for the discovery of bioactive peptides.
