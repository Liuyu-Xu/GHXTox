Abstract. As fundamental signaling molecules, neuropeptides are involved in diverse
22
23
physiological regulatory activities. Abnormal expression patterns have been closely
24
25 linked to neurological and metabolic disorders, which underscores the urgent need for
26
precise and rapid identification methods. Here, we develop NeuroPpred-PSCG, a novel
27
28
multimodal deep learning framework. At its core, the framework employs both a
29
30 bidirectional cross-attention mechanism and a gated fusion module to integrate global
31
semantic insights from the protein language model (ProtT5) with local conformational
32
33
data derived from secondary structure. Furthermore, it utilizes a multi-scale gated
34
35 convolutional network to enhance hierarchical contextual information. On the
36
37 independent test set, NeuroPpred-PSCG surpasses current leading methods on most
38
metrics, achieving ACC of 94.8%, SN of 95.1%, F1-score of 94.8%, and MCC of 0.896,
39
40 reflecting robust overall discriminative capability. Compared to the previous best-
41
42 performing model, NeuroPpred-MSN, our method shows improvements of 1.2% and
43
0.024 in ACC and MCC, respectively, and most notably, a significant 2.8% gain in SN.
44
45
Ablation studies, robustness analysis, and interpretability analysis further substantiate
46
47 the effectiveness of each component, as well as the overall stability and interpretability
48
of NeuroPpred-PSCG. The source code and datasets have been made publicly
49
50
accessible at https://github.com/xinyizhang186/NeuroPpred-PSCG.

4 1 INTRODUCTION
5
6 Neuropeptides are small, bioactive molecules secreted by the nervous system,
7
8 typically ranging from 3 to 100 amino acids in length [1, 2]. As critical signaling
9
mediators, neuropeptides are involved in a wide array of physiological processes,
10
11 including learning and memory, mood regulation, sleep-wake cycles, and energy
12
13 metabolism [3, 4]. Neuropeptide dysregulation is implicated in a spectrum of
14
neurological and metabolic disorders, such as Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease,
15
16 depression, and obesity [5-7]. However, traditional homology-based methods and deep
17
18 learning models designed for other generic bioactive peptides may not generalize well
19
to neuropeptide identification due to their high sequence heterogeneity, variable length,
20
21
and complex post-translational modifications [8-10]. Therefore, developing reliable
22
23 computational methods for efficient, large-scale discovery of neuropeptides is
24
imperative.
25
26
A cornerstone of neuropeptide prediction has been the use of traditional machine
27
28 learning. This approach centers on pairing classical classifiers with manually
29
engineered features. For instance, an early predictor, NeuroPIpred, utilized a support
30
31
vector machine based on four classes of physicochemical features [11]. Subsequent
32
33 work has developed along two principal directions. One path aims to construct more
34
35 sophisticated models through strategies such as stacking and ensemble learning, as
36
illustrated by PredNeuroP’s dual-layer framework [12]. The other path focuses on
37
38 optimizing the input data via feature selection, a technique utilized by NeuroPpred-Fuse
39
40 and NeuroPred-FRL with their single-step and two-step strategies, respectively [13, 14].
41
Despite their straightforward nature, these features can only produce shallow sequence
42
43
representations, thereby failing to capture the deep contextual information required for
44
45 accurate prediction.
46
The paradigm for neuropeptide prediction has recently shifted towards deep learning.
47
48
Liu et al. spearheaded this transition with an early breakthrough, NeuroCNN_GNB.
49
50 Their model, which integrated a convolutional neural network with Gaussian Naive
51
Bayes, was significant for proving the viability of automatic feature extraction [15].
52
53
With the rise of pre-trained protein language models (PLMs), including ESM, BERT,
54
55 and ProtTrans, further progress has been achieved in this field. These approaches
56
57 extract informative latent features from protein and peptide sequences and have been
58
successfully applied to diverse proteomics and peptide-related prediction tasks [16, 17].
59
60 Specifically, although NeuroPred-PLM [18] combined ESM embeddings with a multi-
ACS Paragon Plus Environment

Page 3 of 40 Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling
1
2
3
scale convolutional network, its simplistic feature aggregation scheme limited its ability
4
5 to capture deep sequential dependencies. Likewise, NeuroPred-SVM [19] employed a
6
7 shallow classifier that struggled to fully exploit hierarchical interactions from BERT
8
embeddings. To enrich representations, fusion strategies like NeuroPred-AIMP [20]
9
10 and NeuroPpred-SHE [21] integrated heterogeneous descriptors; however, they
11
12 remained limited by simple feature concatenation, which often introduced redundancy
13
and noise without adaptively selecting optimal signals. Furthermore, even the recent
14
15
state-of-the-art framework, NeuroPpred-MSN [22], which aligned diverse
16
17 representations via a Siamese network and contrastive learning, still lacked an explicit
18
gated filtering mechanism. Collectively, these architectural bottlenecks underscore the
19
20
necessity for a framework that synergizes deep multi-scale context extraction with
21
22 adaptive feature purification.
23
24 To overcome the reliance of existing methods on unimodal feature representations
25
and to mitigate the issue of information redundancy in multi-feature fusion, we propose
26
27 NeuroPpred-PSCG, a novel multimodal deep learning framework. The core of this
28
29 framework is the sophisticated integration of two complementary feature types: (1)
30
Global semantic information, which encodes relationships between distant amino acids
31
32 and evolutionary context using a pre-trained protein language model (ProtT5); (2) Local
33
34 conformational features, which represent the local spatial arrangement of the sequence
35
via predicted secondary structure information. To ensure the effective fusion of this
36
37
heterogeneous information, we design a two-stage integration module. First, a
38
39 bidirectional cross-attention mechanism facilitates deep interaction and alignment
40
between the two feature modalities. Subsequently, a gated fusion mechanism adaptively
41
42
assigns weights to the features, which allows for the dynamic prioritization of
43
44 information most crucial for prediction. On this basis, a multi-scale gated convolutional
45
network is applied to extract contextual patterns from the fused features, thereby
46
47
boosting their expressive capacity. Finally, an attention pooling layer aggregates
48
49 residue-level features into a sequence-level representation for classification.
50
51 NeuroPpred-PSCG achieves competitive performance on the independent test set.
52
Furthermore, extensive ablation studies, robustness analyses, and interpretability
53
54 analyses confirm the model’s effective discriminative ability and its potential for
55
56 practical applications.

2.1 Datasets
7
8
The study employs the public benchmark dataset previously established by Wang et
9
10
al. to ensure a fair and comparable assessment [18]. In their data construction pipeline,
11
12 the positive samples consisted of 11,282 experimentally confirmed neuropeptides
13
14 collected from the NeuroPep 2.0 database [23]. These sequences underwent a two-stage
15
filtering process: first, sequences of length 5 to 100 amino acids were retained;
16
17 subsequently, the CD-HIT tool (version 4.8.1) was utilized to eliminate redundancy at
18
19 a 90% sequence identity cutoff [24], resulting in 4463 non-redundant neuropeptide
20
sequences. For dataset balancing, negative samples were randomly extracted by Wang
21
22 et al. from the UniProt database and then processed using the identical procedure to
23
24 ensure matching length distributions and redundancy removal. In total, the dataset
25
contains 8926 sequences and was divided into training and independent testing subsets
26
27
at a 9:1 split, which we strictly adhere to in our evaluation to ensure an unbiased and
28
29 standard comparison.
30
Additionally, to evaluate the model’s generalization under class-imbalanced
31
32
conditions, we employ two imbalanced datasets from Wen et al. [21]. These datasets
33
34 originate from the same source as the benchmark data but were constructed by the
35
original authors using a more stringent 0.3 similarity cutoff for deduplication, yielding
36
37
454 positive samples and 2,542 negative samples (a ratio of approximately 1:5.6). This
38
39 collection was then partitioned at a 9:1 ratio to form a training dataset and an
40
41 imbalanced testing dataset. The external independent test set was specifically compiled
42
from neuropeptides published after 2023 in the NCBI and PDB databases. Processed
43
44 via the same pipeline, it contains 115 positive and 644 negative samples. Models are
45
46 trained on the imbalanced training set and evaluated on both independent test sets. All
47
datasets are summarized in Table 1.

2.2 Overview of the Model
50
51
52 As illustrated in Figure 1, NeuroPpred-PSCG employs a multimodal deep learning
53
framework to achieve accurate neuropeptide identification. The model’s input layer
54
55 processes two heterogeneous feature modalities in parallel. The first modality utilizes
56
57 the pre-trained protein language model ProtT5 to capture global semantic
58
representations and latent evolutionary patterns from the sequence [25]. The second
59
60

1
2
3
integrates local conformational features derived from the secondary structure, as
4
5 predicted by the PHAT web server [26]. Subsequently, these two modalities undergo
6
7 deep interaction and alignment through a bidirectional cross-attention mechanism. This
8
is followed by a linear gated fusion unit that performs adaptive weighting to emphasize
9
10 the most discriminative information. Based on the fused features, a multi-scale gated
11
12 convolutional network is introduced to model hierarchical contextual dependencies,
13
thereby enhancing their expressive capacity. Finally, an attention pooling layer
14
15
aggregates sequence-level features for the final prediction.
16
17 2.2.1 ProtT5 Semantic Embedding
18
To capture context-aware features from neuropeptide sequences, we utilize the pre-
19
20
trained protein language model ProtT5-XL-UniRef50 (ProtT5) [25]. The model
21
22 contains 33 Transformer layers and is pre-trained on 650 million UniRef50 protein
23
24 sequences via a masked language modeling objective. Through this pre-training, the
25
model can implicitly learn evolutionary information and the relationships between
26
27 sequence, structure, and function. In practice, for any given amino acid sequence S,
28
29 ProtT5 generates a 1024-dimensional embedding for each residue. To align these raw
30
embeddings for the cross-modal interaction module, we employ a linear layer to map
31
32 them into a shared 320-dimensional latent space, based on the hyperparameter
33
34 optimization described below. This procedure significantly reduces computational
35
costs while accentuating the semantic information pertinent to our prediction task.
36
37
2.2.2 Secondary Structure Embedding
38
39 For this work, we employ the PHAT web server to predict the secondary structure of
40
neuropeptide sequences [26]. The PHAT model integrates a powerful pre-trained
41
42
protein language model with a hypergraph-based multi-head attention architecture,
43
44 which effectively transfers semantic information from large proteins to peptide
45
sequences. It can generate predictions in both three-state (H: alpha-helix, E: extended
46
47
beta-strand, C: coil/others) and eight-state (H: alpha-helix, G: 310-helix, I: π-helix, E:
48
49 extended beta-strand, B: isolated beta-strand, T: turn, S: bend, C: coil/others) formats
50
51 [27, 28]. For each generated secondary structure sequence, we first convert it into an
52
initial structural vector using token and positional embedding techniques. Subsequently,
53
54 a linear projection layer maps this structural vector to a dimensional space consistent
55
56 with that of the reduced-dimension ProtT5 embeddings. This step performs feature-
57
dimension alignment before cross-modal fusion and yields the final secondary structure
58
59
representation used by the gated cross-attention module.

2.2.3 Gated Cross-Attention Mechanism
4
5 NeuroPpred-PSCG introduces a gated cross-attention mechanism (Figure 1) to foster
6
7 deep integration between ProtT5 semantic embeddings and secondary structure features
8
[29]. Unlike simple feature addition or concatenation, this mechanism leverages both
9
10 bidirectional cross-attention and a linear gate to enable reciprocal enhancement.
11
12 Specifically, deep semantic information from ProtT5 embeddings strengthens the
13
secondary structure characterization, while local conformational details from the
14
15
secondary structure, in turn, provide the embeddings with rich structural information.
16
17 The features are then passed through the linear gating unit, which dynamically adjusts
18
and combines the information flow from the two modalities for efficient and adaptable
19
20
fusion.
21

23

25

35

33
34 This study compares NeuroPpred-PSCG with eight representative neuropeptide
35

51 worth noting that NeuroPred-FRL reports the highest precision (96.0%), but its
52
sensitivity remains limited (75.7%), implying a higher rate of missed positives. In

54
contrast, NeuroPpred-PSCG achieves the highest sensitivity (95.1%) while maintaining
55
56 a competitive precision (94.6%), resulting in a more balanced trade-off between
57
detecting positive samples and controlling false positives. Furthermore, NeuroPpred-
58
59
PSCG attains an AUROC of 0.980, which is comparable to the best value reported by

19 3.2 Exploration of the NeuroPpred-PSCG Architecture
20
21
To refine the architecture of NeuroPpred-PSCG, we perform a systematic
22
23 hyperparameter analysis covering both the choice of pre-trained backbone and key
24
architectural settings. The initial investigation focuses on four prominent protein
25
26
language models: ESM-2, ProtT5, ProtBERT, and ProtALBERT [25, 35]. As illustrated
27

29

31
ESM-2 ranks second, with ACC of 93.9% and MCC of 0.878. In contrast, ProtBERT
32
33 and ProtALBERT show inferior performance, with ProtBERT exhibiting the most
34

37
38 Using ProtT5 as the backbone, we further tune key architectural hyperparameters.
39
40 Table 3 shows that the projection dimension of 320 achieves the strongest overall
41
performance (ACC: 94.8%, F1-score: 94.8%, MCC: 0.896). Increasing the dimension
42
43
to 384 slightly improves sensitivity (95.7%) but negatively affects overall performance,
44
45 while a larger dimension (512) leads to an additional drop. This suggests that moderate
46

4 3.3 Ablation Experiments
5
6 A series of ablation studies is performed to systematically assess the contributions of
7
8 the key modules within NeuroPpred-PSCG. We benchmark the performance of
9
multiple model variants against the complete model (our baseline) on the independent
10
11 test set, which allows us to verify the efficacy of the multimodal features and the cross-
12
13 modal fusion mechanism.
14
3.3.1 Analysis of the Effectiveness of Multimodal Features
15
16 To investigate the contributions of each feature modality, we design three model
17
18 variants: (i) one using only 3-state secondary structure information (designated as w/o
19
ProtT5, w/ss3); (ii) one using only 8-state secondary structure information (w/o ProtT5,
20
21
w/ss8); and (iii) one using only ProtT5 semantic features (w/o ss8, w/ProtT5). As
22
23 illustrated in Figure 3A and Figure 3C, the model’s performance degrades substantially
24
upon the removal of ProtT5 features when relying solely on either ss3 or ss8
25
26
information. Specifically, the ACC drops to 72.3% and 72.6%, the MCC to 0.449 and
27
28 0.453, and the AUROC to 0.756 and 0.791, respectively. This indicates that structural
29
information alone is insufficient to adequately capture the high-level semantic context
30
31
of neuropeptide sequences. Conversely, when retaining only the ProtT5 semantic
32
33 features, the model’s performance recovers markedly, achieving an ACC of 94.3% and
34
35 an MCC of 0.885. This finding highlights the importance of the pre-trained protein
36
language model in capturing informative sequence patterns. Finally, the full model
37
38 demonstrates superior performance for all indicators, providing strong evidence for the
39

3.7 Limitations and Future Directions
28
29
Despite NeuroPpred-PSCG’s competitive performance, neuropeptide identification
30
31
remains more challenging than predicting other short bioactive peptides. Although
32
33 prediction models developed for antiviral peptides (e.g., TargetAVP-DeepCaps [40]),
34
35 antimicrobial peptides (e.g., deep-AMPpred [41]), and anticancer peptides (e.g., pACP-
36
HybDeep [42]) often report higher evaluation metrics, these methods cannot be directly
37
38 transferred to neuropeptide identification. Neuropeptides typically exhibit high
39
40 sequence heterogeneity, weak evolutionary conservation, limited shared
41
physicochemical motifs, distinct functional mechanisms, and complex post-
42
43
translational processing. These factors complicate identification and suggest that cross-
44
45 task performance comparisons should be interpreted cautiously. Given these challenges,
46
NeuroPpred-PSCG’s balanced performance confirms the effectiveness of integrating
47
48
ProtT5-derived semantics with structural information via adaptive cross-modal fusion.
49
50 Nevertheless, several limitations remain and should be addressed in future work. Our
51
future efforts will focus on the following four aspects: (1) Integration of 3D structural
52
53
information: The current model is built upon sequence information and secondary
54
55 structure predictions, without explicitly modeling the 3D conformations that are closely
56
57 linked to biological function. Introducing structural data predicted by AlphaFold2 [43]
58
or ESMFold [44] may help capture spatial arrangements and functional domains more
59
60 effectively, which could contribute to improved prediction accuracy. (2) Generalization
ACS Paragon Plus Environment

===== PAGE 18 =====
Page 17 of 40 Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling
1
2
3
under external imbalanced conditions: While NeuroPpred-PSCG maintains competitive
4
5 overall performance on the external imbalanced test set, its sensitivity drops under
6
7 external distribution shifts. This suggests that identifying positive neuropeptides
8
remains challenging despite comparable class ratios, possibly due to variations in
9
10 sequence composition and data sources. Future work will explore threshold calibration
11
12 and cost-sensitive learning to enhance sensitivity and real-world robustness. (3)
13
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT): The present study relies on static ProtT5
14
15
embeddings, without further adapting the pre-trained model to the target task. Applying
16
17 PEFT methods, such as LoRA [45], could enable efficient fine-tuning with a limited
18
number of additional parameters. This could enhance its ability to capture task-relevant
19
20
patterns in neuropeptide sequences, while preserving efficiency in computation and
21
22 memory usage. (4) Generalization to a wider range of bioactive peptides: NeuroPpred-
23
24 PSCG has demonstrated considerable versatility. We plan to extend to other bioactive
25
peptide tasks, such as antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory peptide prediction, to
26
27 explore its potential as a general-purpose framework.
28
29 4 CONCLUSIONS
30
31
This study introduces NeuroPpred-PSCG, a multimodal deep learning framework for
32
33 neuropeptide identification. The model architecture integrates information across
34
35 different scales. Through the application of a bidirectional cross-attention mechanism
36
and a multi-scale gated convolutional network, it harmonizes the high-level semantic
37
38 insights captured by the ProtT5 language model with the localized structural patterns
39
40 defined by secondary structures, thus facilitating a hierarchical analysis of the sequence.
41
Our method achieves consistent improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods
42
43
in overall performance. Moreover, ablation results highlight the importance of the
44
45 multimodal fusion strategy for model performance. Additional evaluations of
46
robustness and generalization, together with visualization analyses, show that the model
47
48
remains stable and captures biologically meaningful patterns. Overall, this study
49
50 highlights the potential of multimodal deep fusion in characterizing complex biological
51
sequences, offering a promising approach for the discovery of bioactive peptides.
