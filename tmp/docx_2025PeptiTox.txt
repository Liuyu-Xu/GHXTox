ABSTRACT: Peptide toxicity prediction is a critical task in biomedical research, inﬂuencing drug safety and therapeutic
development. Traditional methods, relying on sequence similarity or handcrafted features, struggle to capture the complex
relationship between peptide structure and toxicity. In this study, we propose PeptiTox, an advanced deep learning framework that
integrates protein language models (PLMs) and geometric deep learning to enhance peptide toxicity prediction. Speciﬁcally, ESM2
is employed to extract sequence embeddings, while ESMFold predicts the three-dimensional (3D) peptide structure. The structural
information is then transformed into a graph representation, where residues serve as nodes, and interactions between residues form
edges. A graph neural network (GNN) is subsequently used to learn peptide representations and classify their toxicity. Experimental
results demonstrate that PeptiTox signiﬁcantly outperforms state-of-the-art models across multiple evaluation metrics. Our ﬁndings
highlight the importance of integrating sequence and structural knowledge for peptide toxicity prediction, paving the way for safer
and more eﬀective peptide-based therapeutics.

IV.II. Ablation Study on the Architectural Compo-
nents of PeptiTox. To explore the optimal architecture of
our model and demonstrate that each module is both eﬀective
and nonredundant, we conducted ablation experiments. In
these experiments, we systematically removed or replaced one
module at a time while keeping the rest of the model
unchanged. Speciﬁcally, we performed the following experi-
ments: (1) To evaluate the contribution of the ESM2 model,
we constructed a model variant termed w/o ESM2, in which
the ESM2 module was removed from the architecture,
retaining only the peptide’s 3D structure and geometric
features. (2) To assess the importance of geometric features,
we implemented a model variant termed w/o geometric
features, in which node representations were constructed using
only the sequence features extracted by ESM2, while all
geometric information was omitted. (3) To examine the role of
structural information, we designed another variant, referred to
as w/o structure, where the structural input was removed and
only sequence features from ESM2 were utilized. To maintain
model functionality in the absence of structural data, we
replaced the graph neural network with LSTM and CNN
layers. We evaluated the performance of the proposed model
and its variants across six metrics: Accuracy, F1-score, MCC,
Sensitivity, Speciﬁcity, and FDR. The experimental results are
presented in Table 1. From the experimental results, we can
observe that the removal of the ESM2 model leads to a
substantial decline in performance across all six evaluation
metrics. Speciﬁcally, Accuracy drops from 0.9444 to 0.9009 (a
decrease of 4.6%), F1-score from 0.9439 to 0.8993 (4.7%),
MCC from 0.8888 to 0.8023 (9.7%), Sensitivity from 0.9378
to 0.8856 (5.6%), Speciﬁcity from 0.9509 to 0.9162 (3.6%),
and FDR increases from 0.0499 to 0.0863 (73%). These results
highlight the critical role of the pretrained protein language
model in capturing sequence information and signiﬁcantly
improving the model’s ability to distinguish between toxic and
nontoxic peptides. Similarly, removing structural information

V. CONCLUSIONS
In this study, we introduced PeptiTox, a novel deep learning-
based framework that integrates protein language models and
geometric deep learning for peptide toxicity prediction. Our
results demonstrate that incorporating both sequence embed-
dings from ESM2 and structural information from ESMFold
leads to superior predictive performance compared to existing
methods. Ablation experiments further conﬁrm that sequence,
structural, and geometric features collectively contribute to
model performance, with sequence embeddings playing a
dominant role. Additionally, our comparison of diﬀerent graph
neural network architectures highlights the eﬀectiveness of
Transformer-based GNNs in capturing intricate peptide
relationships.
In summary, the proposed model eﬀectively learns the
distinguishing features of toxic and nontoxic peptides and
progressively enhances its discrimination ability through
hierarchical learning.
Despite the promising performance of our approach, there is
still room for improvement. First, the model lacks interpret-
ability, making it unclear which speciﬁc patterns in peptide
sequences contribute to their toxicity. Second, our study
focuses solely on peptide toxicity prediction and does not
extend to other properties such as biological activity. In future
work, we will enhance the model’s interpretability by
identifying key sequence patterns associated with toxicity.
Additionally, we will expand the model’s capabilities to enable
multiattribute prediction, allowing for a more comprehensive
assessment of peptide properties.
